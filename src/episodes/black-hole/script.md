## Science notes and assumptions
- **Mass and location:** the black hole has Earth's mass and appears at rest relative to Earth, at the Moon's distance (384,400 km). Its Schwarzschild radius is 8.9 mm, so it's about 1.8 cm across: marble-sized.
- **Invisible:** with nothing falling in, it emits nothing. Its lensing (Einstein) radius against background stars is about 1–7 arcseconds, far below what the naked eye can resolve (about 60″). Telescopes would see it; eyes wouldn't.
- **Free fall:** two equal masses fall toward each other and meet halfway. Free-fall time from rest is (π/2)·√(r³ / 2GM_total) = **3.43 days (3 d 10 h 22 m)**. Everyone falls with the planet, so the fall itself isn't felt; only tidal stretching is.
- **Distance over time:**

  | Time | Distance | Tides (× the Moon's) |
  |---|---|---|
  | day 0 | 384,400 km | ×81 |
  | day 2 | 297,000 km | ×176 |
  | day 2.81 | 192,000 km (halfway) | ×654 |
  | day 3 | 155,000 km | ×1,240 |
  | day 3.2 | 106,000 km | ×3,900 |
  | day 3.4 | 29,000 km | ×180,000 |

- **Tides:** tidal force scales with M/d³. At the same distance as the Moon, Earth's mass is 81× the Moon's, so the tides are 81× stronger.
- **The second half of the fall:** half the distance is covered by day 2.81, so the second half takes about **15 hours** (897 minutes).
- **Torn apart:** Earth's fluid Roche limit for an Earth-mass companion is about 2.44 R⊕ ≈ 15,500 km. The black hole crosses it about **17 minutes** before reaching the surface. Within about one Earth radius of the surface, its direct pull is stronger than Earth's surface gravity.
- **The Moon:** a three-body interaction with two Earth masses. Ejection or a chaotic new orbit are both plausible, so the chapter is tagged DRAMATISED.
- **Millions of years:** at the Eddington limit for an Earth-mass black hole (about 4×10²⁵ W, roughly 10% of the Sun's output), accreting an Earth mass at 10% efficiency takes about 45 million years. Super-Eddington accretion could be faster, so the chapter is tagged HYPOTHESIS.
- **Closest known black hole:** Gaia BH1, about 1,560 light-years away (discovered 2022). Re-check for newer discoveries before publishing.
- **Ignored:** Earth's orbit around the Sun (the pair keeps orbiting together) and Earth's rotation (it sets the twice-a-day tide rhythm).
